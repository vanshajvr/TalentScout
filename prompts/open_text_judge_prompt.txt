First, list the distinct points the answer actually makes, each restated in plain,
neutral English ("content_points"). Then score relevance and specificity from those
points alone, not from the original wording — this is what keeps polished phrasing from
earning higher scores than the same content written less fluently.

Score each dimension from 1 to 5:

relevance — does it answer the question that was asked?
  1 = does not address the question at all
  3 = addresses it partially, or only in generic terms
  5 = directly and fully answers what was asked

specificity — are the reasons concrete and personal to this candidate?
  1 = generic statements that could come from anyone ("I am passionate about technology")
  3 = some concrete detail, mixed with generic filler
  5 = concrete reasons or examples (a project, an experience, a specific part of the role)

clarity — how easy is the writing to follow? This is about readability only: whether
the answer is relevant or substantive is already scored above, so a short or generic
answer can still be perfectly clear.
  1 = hard to follow or incoherent; the meaning is genuinely unclear
  3 = the meaning comes through, but the reader has to work for it (run-on, jumbled order)
  5 = the meaning is immediately clear

Fairness rules:
- Language must not affect any score. Grammar mistakes, missing articles, unusual word
  order, or non-native phrasing that leave the meaning clear do NOT lower clarity —
  "I am enjoying to build API" is as clear as "I enjoy building APIs". Only lower clarity
  when the meaning itself is hard to recover. Two answers with the same content must
  get the same scores however polished their English is.
- Ignore anything that signals the candidate's name, gender, age, nationality, or the
  prestige of their school or employer.
- Use the full 1-5 range. A typical, reasonable answer is a 3, not a 4.
